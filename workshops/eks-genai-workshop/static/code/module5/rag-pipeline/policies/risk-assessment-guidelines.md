# Risk Assessment Guidelines

**Policy ID:** RISK-2025-v2
**Effective Date:** 2025-01-01
**Owner:** Credit Risk — Model & Policy Governance

## 1. Purpose

These guidelines define how underwriters and automated agents assign a risk tier
to each application and what actions each tier requires.

## 2. Risk Scoring Inputs

Risk is assessed from documented financial factors only:

- Credit score (FICO) and credit history depth
- Debt-to-income (DTI) ratio
- Income stability and employment tenure
- Loan amount relative to income
- Document authenticity and completeness

## 3. Risk Tiers

| Tier | Criteria | Action |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Low** | FICO ≥ 700, DTI ≤ 36%, clean documents | Eligible for auto-approval |
| **Moderate** | FICO 660–699 or DTI 37–43% | Manual review |
| **Elevated** | FICO 620–659, or document-authenticity concerns | Manual review + senior sign-off |
| **High** | FICO < 620, DTI > 43%, or unverifiable income | Decline |

## 4. Document Authenticity

Document-authenticity concerns (non-standard formatting, typed signatures,
suspiciously round figures, template-like appearance) raise the risk tier to at
least **Elevated** and require manual review. They are a review trigger, not an
automatic decline. The final decision must still rest on verified financial
criteria.

## 5. Fair Lending

Risk assessment must never incorporate protected-class characteristics (race,
color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, or receipt of public
assistance). Any factor used must be financial and documented, consistent with
ECOA and the Fair Housing Act. Inconsistent standards across applicants are
prohibited.

## 6. Decision Rationale

Every decision must record: the assigned risk tier, the financial factors that
drove it, any compensating factors, and explicit citations to the governing
policy sections.
