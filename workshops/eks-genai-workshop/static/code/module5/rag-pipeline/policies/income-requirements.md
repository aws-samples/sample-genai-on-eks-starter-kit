# Income and Debt-to-Income Requirements

**Policy ID:** INC-2025-v1
**Effective Date:** 2025-01-01
**Owner:** Credit Risk — Underwriting Standards

## 1. Income Verification

Income must be verified before any approval. Acceptable evidence:

- **W-2 employees:** Two most recent pay stubs **or** most recent W-2.
- **Self-employed:** Two years of tax returns plus year-to-date profit-and-loss.
- **Other income** (rental, retirement, benefits): Official statements covering
  the most recent three months.

Income that cannot be documented may **not** be counted toward qualification.

## 2. Debt-to-Income (DTI) Calculation

```
DTI = (Total monthly debt obligations) / (Gross monthly income)
```

Monthly debt obligations include: existing loan payments, minimum credit-card
payments, rent or mortgage, and the estimated payment for the requested loan.
They exclude: utilities, insurance, and discretionary spending.

## 3. DTI Thresholds

| DTI ratio | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| ≤ 36% | Strong — eligible for auto-approval if other criteria met |
| 37% – 43% | Acceptable — manual review required |
| > 43% | Exceeds policy — auto-approval prohibited; decline unless compensating factors documented |

## 4. Compensating Factors

A DTI above the standard threshold may be offset only by **documented** factors:
significant liquid reserves (≥ 6 months of payments), a FICO score ≥ 760, or a
loan-to-value position that materially reduces risk. Compensating factors must be
recorded in the decision rationale.

## 5. Worked Example

An applicant earning $75,000/year has a gross monthly income of $6,250. With
$275 in existing monthly debt and an estimated $150 loan payment, monthly
obligations total $425. DTI = 425 / 6,250 = **6.8%**, which is well within the
≤ 36% strong band.
